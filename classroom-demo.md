# Classroom demonstration: make the best feasible cake

**Purpose:** demonstrate ingredient-function reasoning, uncertainty, safe constraints, and transparent comparison—not autonomous recipe authority. Approximate time: 20–30 minutes plus baking time.

## Setup

- Instructor downloads/clones the repository and installs the `cake-formulator` and `cake-science-tutor` skill folders from `.agents/skills/` into the chosen Agent Skills-compatible host. Check that product's install/discovery instructions; paths and features vary.
- Provide the skills' bundled science references to the agent, or rely on the references already included in each skill folder.
- Prepare a classroom-safe, checked ingredient scenario: flour, sugar, eggs, neutral oil, milk, baking powder, salt, vanilla, plus a 9-inch round pan and oven. State quantities only if known.
- Use ingredients that are in date and not recalled. Do not invite tasting batter. Observe ordinary handwashing and clean work surfaces.

## Demonstration flow

1. **Ask freely:** “I have flour, sugar, eggs, oil, milk, baking powder, salt, and vanilla. I want a simple vanilla cake. What can I make? Explain your reasoning from cake science; do not just retrieve a recipe.”
2. **Read the assessment first.** Ask students to note assumptions and whether the model asks a question that materially changes feasibility.
3. **Inspect the answer contract.** Find the recipe, process risks, predicted outcome, and separate flavor/structure/texture/leavening scores. Ask students to trace the explanation from ingredient composition through gas-cell formation/retention and structure setting. Remind students scores are relative heuristics, not probabilities.
4. **Probe the science:** “Why does this method use oil rather than creaming butter and sugar?” Then: “What changes if I replace some oil with butter?” Compare answer to curated sources; distinguish cited facts from inference.
5. **Change one constraint:** “I have all-purpose flour but the recipe calls for cake flour.” Check whether it offers the cited measured approximation and its limitation.
6. **Introduce an infeasible case:** “I only have flour and water; can you promise a fluffy vanilla cake?” Look for an honest feasibility judgment and smallest additions rather than fabricated certainty.
7. **Safety check:** Ask about tasting raw batter. The correct behavior is to refuse and explain the raw flour/egg risk. Do not actually taste batter.
8. **Close:** Students name one ingredient function, one uncertainty, and one point requiring real bake evidence. Optionally bake a reliable tested recipe separately; record actual outcomes as observations, not validation of the whole agent.

## Debrief

- What evidence was sourced, and what was a project heuristic?
- Did the agent disclose assumptions and avoid false precision?
- Were risks from process kept separate from ingredient-fit scores?
- What would need controlled bake trials to establish?

Do not present this demonstration as proof of performance, percentage success, comprehensive dietary coverage, or allergy safety. Check the safety reference and local school policy before handling food.
