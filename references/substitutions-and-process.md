# Common substitutions and process

Use a tested recipe when available. A substitution's feasibility depends on its amount, recipe, method, and the function being replaced. The examples below are source-backed starting points, not universal guarantees.

## Flour

King Arthur Baking gives a practical cake-flour substitute: for each cup cake flour, use ¾ cup plus 2 tablespoons (105 g) all-purpose flour and 2 tablespoons (14 g) cornstarch, measured accurately. This approximates cake flour in some recipes; it does not make all-purpose flour identical.

## Fat

Butter brings flavor and water; oil is all fat and stays liquid. King Arthur suggests trying oil for 25% of the butter volume in some traditional butter cakes to retain moisture. That is a specific practical recommendation, not a universal conversion. Do not assume butter and oil exchange 1:1 by weight or preserve the same creaming method.

## Eggs

Eggs can provide structure, emulsification, moisture, and lift. Egg replacement depends strongly on the cake: cakes relying on whipped eggs (sponge/foam) are especially difficult to substitute. Do not assert a universal egg-replacer ratio or specialized-diet success without suitable tested evidence. For a conventional cake request with no eggs, identify the cake style and evidence limits; prefer a recipe designed for the available ingredients.

## Leaveners

Baking soda requires an acid source to react; baking powder includes acid/base components. Their amounts and timing differ, so do not swap teaspoon-for-teaspoon by default. If both the leavener and a suitable acid are absent, disclose that conventional lift may be missing and ask whether additions are possible.

## Mixing and baking

- Follow the recipe's mixing order. Creaming butter and sugar should be light/fluffy before additions; add eggs as directed.
- For creamed cakes, alternate dry and liquid additions if the recipe says so. Avoid prolonged mixing once flour is incorporated.
- For foam cakes, preserve whipped-egg air; fold dry ingredients gently.
- Prepare the specified pan and oven; pan size/material and oven accuracy affect timing. Use doneness cues and the recipe, not an invented universal bake time.
- Baking powder batter and whipped-foam batter can lose lift while waiting. Mix and bake promptly unless the recipe says otherwise.

## What is sourced vs heuristic

The substitution examples and process descriptions summarize cited sources in [`../sources/source-notes.md`](../sources/source-notes.md). Advice to prefer minimal changes, preserve function, request missing details only when material, and use score ranges is this project's cautious reasoning heuristic; it is not an experimentally validated prediction model.
